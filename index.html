GPS Surveyor – Privacy Policy

App: GPS Surveyor: Routes & Points (package com.markogola.gpstracker)
Effective date: 17 November 2026

GPS Surveyor shows your GPS coordinates, lets you save waypoints and record routes, lets you map land parcels and produce survey scheme plans, and lets you export all of these. This policy explains what information the app uses and where it goes. In short: your location data stays on your phone. The app has no accounts and no analytics, and the developer does not receive your data. The app shows ads served by Google AdMob, described below.

Information the app uses

  • Precise and approximate location. Used to show your current coordinates, to save waypoints you choose to save, and to record routes you start. Location is only used while the app is open, or while a route recording you started is running (shown by a notification with a Stop button). The app does not request background location access.
  • Waypoints and routes you save. Coordinates, altitude, accuracy, time and any names or notes you type. These are stored only in the app's private storage on your device.
  • Survey projects you create. Project details you type (such as project name, reference, client or landowner name, county, parcel number, surveyor name and dates), boundary points with their original GPS observations and any edits, mapped features, subdivision plots and drawing settings. These are stored only in the app's private storage on your device.
  • Feature photos you add. When you attach a photo to a mapped feature, the app opens your phone's camera app or photo picker and keeps a copy of the chosen picture in its private storage. The app does not request camera or storage permission and does not read other photos.
  • Files you import. When you import coordinates (CSV, TXT, GPX or KML), the app reads only the file you pick in Android's file picker, on your device.
  • External GNSS receivers (optional). If you connect a Bluetooth GNSS or RTK receiver, the app reads the position data (NMEA) it sends and uses it like the phone's own GPS. The app reads the names of devices already paired with your phone only to let you choose one, and does not scan for other nearby devices. Nothing is sent from the app to the receiver, and no Bluetooth data leaves your phone. If you use a receiver through another app set as Android's mock location, GPS Surveyor only notes that a position came from such a source.
  • App settings such as your preferred coordinate format and recording interval, stored on your device.

What leaves your device

  • Nothing is sent to the developer. The app has no server and the developer collects no personal data.
  • Ads (Google AdMob). The app shows a banner and occasional full-screen ads from Google AdMob. To serve and measure ads and prevent fraud, Google's ad SDK collects your device's advertising ID, IP address (from which an approximate location can be inferred), and information about ad interactions, app performance and crashes. Your saved waypoints, routes and precise GPS position are never given to the ad SDK. Where the law requires it (for example in the EEA and UK), the app asks for your consent first, and you can change your choice at any time with "Ad privacy choices" on the My location screen. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services.
  • Exports you choose to make. When you tap Share or Save, the app creates a file (GPX, KML, KMZ, CSV, GeoJSON, and for survey projects also PDF and DXF) and hands it to the app or folder you pick (for example email, WhatsApp, Google Drive or your Downloads folder). What happens next is governed by that app or service.
  • Map tiles. To draw maps, the app downloads map images from OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org) or OpenTopoMap (opentopomap.org). Like any web request, these servers receive your device's IP address and the map area being viewed. They do not receive your GPS coordinates from the app. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy.
  • Google Play services location. On devices with Google Play services, the app uses Google's location service to improve accuracy. If "Google Location Accuracy" is turned on in your phone's settings, Google may process Wi-Fi and mobile-network signals as described in Google's privacy policy. You control this setting on your phone.

Data retention and deletion

Saved waypoints and routes stay on your device until you delete them in the app (Saved → item → Delete). Survey projects, with their points, features, photos, plots and drawing settings, stay until you delete the project (Survey → project → Delete). Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android settings, deletes all of its data. Because the developer never receives your data, there is nothing for the developer to delete.

Permissions

  • Location (precise/approximate): core functionality described above.
  • Notifications: to show the ongoing notification while a route is being recorded.
  • Foreground service (location): so a recording you start keeps working with the screen off.
  • Internet / network state: to download map tiles and ads.
  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth connect) on Android 12 and later, or Bluetooth on older versions: only to connect to a GNSS receiver you choose. It is requested only when you open the receiver option, and the app works fully without it.
  • Advertising ID (added by the Google ad SDK): to show and measure ads.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Security

Data is kept in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read. Exported files are only created when you ask for them.
